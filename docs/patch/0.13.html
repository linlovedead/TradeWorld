We sat a "newcomer" down at the game — someone who has never played an economic strategy — and watched where they would quit. They quit at minute nine: the shop's first month closed deep in the red, cards about wars in faraway countries kept popping up, and the play button ran time at an hour a second. 0.13 fixes that.

The first 15 minutes

  • ✚ An honest first month. Goods the shop stocked ahead and still has on its shelves are not a loss. The finances show them as "Goods on the shelves", and the first month now closes in the black when it really is.
  • ✚ Play means play. The ▶ button and space resume at the last speed you picked (a day a second at first), not an hour a second.
  • ✚ A near goal. "100 thousand of profit in a year", "a second building", "a million in the cash" — in your card and at the end of the first chapter. The tutorial offers a plant of your own when you can afford it, and an upgrade of the shop before that.
  • ✚ One piece of advice, not two. The tutorial and the advisor no longer argue about the markup.
  • ✚ Less noise. Tensions that already existed in 2000 are not news; a regime change, an ultimatum or a war in a faraway country no longer pauses the game and goes to the feed.

A weekly rhythm

  • ✚ Weekly results. Every building shows its profit by week: a "by week / by month" switch above the chart in its finances, and "/wk" in the top bar is a real week.
  • ✚ The world lives every day. Harvest failures, war rumors, sanctions and revolutions come on different days of the month, not all on the first.
  • ✚ Rivals answer sooner. A chain that loses buyers to your shop starts cutting its prices within a week.

A quick city menu

A click on the map opens the city's menu, like a settlement in Mount & Blade 2: the city itself, "Build here", your buildings there with their troubles, the country's market, the province, the country and its government.
